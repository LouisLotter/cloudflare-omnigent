# Sessions and sharing

## Your sessions run on your computer

A session is one conversation with an agent and the work it does. When you start one, you choose which of your computers it runs on (the **Host** choice when you start it). The agent works there, with the Claude or Codex login on that computer: your subscription. Its files stay on your computer, and its usage counts against your plan, as if you had run the agent on your own laptop.

The list of sessions you see is your own, plus any that someone has shared with you. Other members cannot see your sessions unless you share them, with one exception: owners and administrators of the team (below).

## Sharing a session

Open the session's menu at the top and choose **Share**. Search for a colleague and give them one of two levels:

- **Read** lets them follow the session: its conversation and its results. They cannot send it anything. They can browse the session's files only if you also turn on **Workspace files**, which is off by default because files often hold things not meant for everyone.
- **Edit** lets them take part. They can send messages into the session, and each message they send is a turn the agent runs on your computer, with your subscription and with the connections the session uses. They can see its files and answer its questions, the same as you.

The Share dialog says this too: "Someone with Edit can run turns on this session's computer, with its owner's subscription." Give Edit only to people you would let use your computer and your account, because that is what it amounts to. An agent that takes instructions from a colleague can read what your computer holds.

You can change a level or take it away at any time in the same dialog (the bin icon next to their name). People can be added only if they are members of the team. Nothing can be shared by link or made public: the installation does not allow it.

## Owners and administrators can see members' sessions

Owners and administrators of the team can open every member's sessions, shared or not, and Omnigent lets them act there as the session's owner could. This is how Omnigent works today, and the team has chosen to keep it. Keep it in mind for anything you would not want them to see.

## Whose subscription a turn uses

The turn always runs where the session lives: on its owner's computer, with its owner's subscription. When a colleague with Edit sends a message into your session, it is your subscription that does the work. When you send a message into a session someone shared with you, it is theirs. To run something with your own subscription, start your own session on your own computer.

## A warning about the providers' terms

Claude, ChatGPT and other subscriptions are sold to one person, under terms that person accepted. Sharing a session with Edit, or otherwise letting someone else drive an agent that runs on your subscription, may break Claude's terms, and possibly other providers' terms too. Read-only sharing is less of a concern, but it is still your account's output. Check the terms that apply to your own subscription before you share: Anthropic's [Claude Code legal and compliance](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance) page, and OpenAI's terms of use for ChatGPT and Codex. You and each colleague are responsible for checking this yourselves.

This is a warning, not legal advice. The installation does not decide it for you, and it does not stop you from sharing.
